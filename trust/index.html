What the Verified badge means

Every event on Eventible passes an automated legality and image check before it can go live. Anything flagged waits for a person, with a target of 24 hours to a decision.

The checks every event passes

Legality declaration

The organiser confirms the event complies with local law and that any required permits or insurance are in place — an alcohol tasting licence, a food hygiene certificate, a guide licence for protected natural areas.

Text screening

Title and description are screened against prohibited categories: weapons, drugs, gambling, multi-level marketing, unlicensed medical or financial advice, hate or extremist content, sexual services, and events involving minors without safeguarding details.

Image screening

Every uploaded image is scanned before it is stored, for sexual content, nudity, violence, gore, hate symbols and drugs. All uploads are also hash-matched against known child sexual abuse material. EXIF data is stripped and originals are never served publicly.

Risk scoring

New organisers in their first three events, higher-risk categories, and anything the text screen flags always go to a human moderator. Everything else publishes immediately.

The record behind every organiser

You should be able to book someone you have never met. That only works if the history is visible, so every organiser profile shows events held, attendees served, average rating, cancellation rate and verification badges — plus every past event with the reviews it received. Cancelled events are shown as cancelled, never hidden.

Reviews come only from people who held a valid or checked-in ticket. An organiser may reply once to a review. They cannot edit or remove one. A review is hidden only after a moderator decision, and that decision is logged.

Organisers with fewer than three completed events carry a New organiser label, so you know the record is still short.

If something is wrong

Any logged-in user can report an event. Three independent reports unpublish it automatically pending review. Moderation decisions follow a strike system — warning, a seven-day publishing suspension, then a permanent ban — and illegal content skips straight to a ban. Every decision is logged with the reviewer, the time and the reason, and can be appealed once.

If an event did not take place or was materially different from its description, you can open a dispute within seven days. The organiser has five days to respond, and Eventible decides.
